## Executive Summary

The Intermediate Zoom Out Review evaluated `docs/designs/support_orchestrator.md` (lines 1..399) from high altitude against the Zoom Out Standard, taking into account the prior findings from Stage 5 (Initial Zoom Out), Stage 6 (Comprehension Test), and Stage 7 (Critic Review).

The architecture remains strongly aligned with root problem goals (modular monolith, unified retrieval, dual-gate routing, fault-tolerant exception containment). However, several speculative features flagged in the initial audit (MLflow tracking, redundant CLI scaffolding, and confidence blending) remain unpurged. Furthermore, scrutiny of the Critic and Comprehension stages reveals risks of secondary over-engineering: specifically, introducing an elaborate token-bucket rate limiter, copying code-level Pydantic/DDL weeds into the design document, and over-complicating the offline fallback mock.

Below are the architectural findings and simplicity constraints to enforce in the final design revision.

---

## Detailed Findings

### Finding 1: Speculative MLflow Tracking & Dead Code Invariant
* **Severity:** 🔴 Blocker
* **Affected Scope:** Section *Goals* (lines 34–35), *Architectural Topology* (lines 127–128), *Directory Scaffolding* (line 180), *Audit Logging* (lines 268–269), *Decisions & Rejected Alternatives* (lines 278–281), and *Implementation Plan* (line 367).
* **Simplicity Rationale:** 
  Non-Goal NG6 and Rejected Alternative #2 explicitly reject distributed tracing and daemon-based observability. Yet the document still carries `evaluation/tracker.py`, an optional `--track` flag, an MLflow Mermaid node, and text describing lazy MLflow logging. Moreover, `mlflow` is completely absent from `requirements.txt`. Append-only SQLite logging (`data/decision_log.db`) and local JSON metrics reports generated by `evaluation/metrics.py` satisfy 100% of Acceptance Criteria A8, A9, and A10. Retaining MLflow as an "optional hook" violates Zoom-Out Standard §2 (YAGNI) and creates an architectural contradiction.
  *Resolution*: Completely purge all MLflow references, `--track` flags, and `evaluation/tracker.py` from the design. SQLite and local JSON metrics are the sole persistence and evaluation mechanisms.

---

### Finding 2: Redundant Public Execution Interface (`src/cli.py`)
* **Severity:** 🟡 Major
* **Affected Scope:** Section *Streamlined Directory Scaffolding* (line 140) and *Target Files to Create/Modify* (lines 361–363).
* **Simplicity Rationale:** 
  The Build Specification (§04, lines 327–339) defines exactly two operational entrypoints: `src.api` (FastAPI REST service) and `evaluation.harness` (unattended batch evaluation CLI), alongside the test suite (`pytest tests/ -v`). Adding an interactive single-ticket CLI (`src/cli.py`) creates an unneeded public interface that duplicates pipeline execution paths and increases test maintenance without addressing any requirement. Single tickets can be verified via the REST API or by passing a single-item JSON file to `evaluation.harness`.
  *Resolution*: Eliminate `src/cli.py` from directory scaffolding and implementation plans.

---

### Finding 3: Conflicting Calibration Logic (Blended Score vs Orthogonal Dual Gate)
* **Severity:** 🟡 Major
* **Affected Scope:** Section 6 *Streamlined 3-Tier Provider Cascade & Calibration* (line 252) vs Section 5 *Dual-Gate Routing Logic* (line 241).
* **Simplicity Rationale:** 
  Line 241 defines a clean, deterministic routing invariant:
  $$\text{Action} = \text{AUTO\_RESPOND} \iff c \ge 0.75 \land s_{\text{ret}} \ge 0.65 \land \text{urgency} \neq \text{CRITICAL} \land \neg \text{must\_not\_auto\_respond}$$
  In contrast, line 252 asserts that *"routing confidence blends model probability with semantic retrieval similarity ($s_{\text{ret}}$) and heuristic validation checks."* Blending $s_{\text{ret}}$ into classification confidence $c$ couples intent classification to retrieval and double-counts the retrieval similarity score at the routing gate.
  *Resolution*: Remove the blending language from line 252. Maintain classification confidence $c \in [0.0, 1.0]$ as an orthogonal intent certainty score, allowing the dual-gate router to evaluate $c$ and $s_{\text{ret}}$ independently.

### Finding 4: Linear Pipeline Description Contradicts Conditional Graph Branching
* **Severity:** 🟡 Major
* **Affected Scope:** Section 10 *Detailed Implementation Plan* (line 358) vs Mermaid Diagram (lines 103–128) and CUJ 2 (lines 81–85).
* **Simplicity Rationale:** 
  Line 358 describes the graph assembly as: *"LangGraph StateGraph compilation connecting Ingest $\rightarrow$ Classify $\rightarrow$ Retrieve $\rightarrow$ Route $\rightarrow$ Generate / Escalate $\rightarrow$ Guardrails $\rightarrow$ Audit."* This linear notation suggests that escalated tickets pass through `Guardrails`, which contradicts CUJ 2 and the Mermaid diagram where escalated tickets bypass both `generation` and `guardrails` directly to `Audit`.
  *Resolution*: Clarify the conditional edge topologies in Section 10: `Route` branches to `generation_node` (for `AUTO_RESPOND`) or directly to `escalation_builder_node` (for `ESCALATE`). Guardrail verification runs exclusively on auto-response drafts before merging at the audit logger.

### Finding 5: Over-Engineered Rate Limiting Proposal (Critic DES-1 Scrutiny)
* **Severity:** 🟡 Major
* **Affected Scope:** Critic Report Finding DES-1 (recommending explicit token-bucket rate limiters in `src/generation/cascade.py` and `evaluation/harness.py`).
* **Simplicity Rationale:** 
  Critic DES-1 identified a genuine operational risk: free-tier HTTP 429 rate limits collapsing the cascade during the 120-ticket unattended batch run. However, the recommended mitigation—building stateful token-bucket rate limiter classes across multiple modules—is a classic premature optimization and altitude drop. In an in-process batch modular monolith, rate limit mitigation requires only two simple, localized mechanisms:
  1. A configurable inter-ticket delay in `evaluation/harness.py` (`time.sleep(request_delay)`).
  2. Standard exponential backoff with jitter on HTTP 429 status codes within the provider HTTP client wrapper.
  *Resolution*: Reject dedicated token-bucket rate limiter abstractions. Specify pacing via configurable batch sleep intervals and standard retry-after / backoff logic in the provider cascade.

#### Finding 6: Altitude Drop & Implementation Weeds in Data Models and DDL (Comprehension Findings 2 & 8 Scrutiny)
* **Severity:** 🟡 Major
* **Affected Scope:** Comprehension Report Findings 2 and 8 (demanding full Pydantic class definitions and SQL DDL schemas in the design document).
* **Simplicity Rationale:** 
  Demanding full Python dataclass/Pydantic class declarations and raw SQL DDL migration scripts within the high-level architecture document violates Zoom-Out Standard §3 (Anti-Weeds). The architectural document must establish boundary contracts and invariants, not copy-paste lines of implementation code.
  *Resolution*: Provide concise structural schema tables for core models (`CanonicalTicket`, `DocumentChunk`, `EscalationPacket`, `ProcessedTicketResult`) and the SQLite `decision_log` table (listing field names, types, nullability, and index keys). Do not bloat the design document with concrete Python class bodies or SQL `CREATE TABLE` scripts.

### Finding 7: Over-Complication Risk in Tertiary Offline Heuristic Mock (Comprehension Finding 11 Scrutiny)
* **Severity:** 🟡 Major
* **Affected Scope:** Section 6 (lines 250–251), Implementation Plan (line 345), and Comprehension Finding 11.
* **Simplicity Rationale:** 
  Comprehension Finding 11 questions whether the offline heuristic mock should synthesize customer answers. The tertiary offline mock exists solely to guarantee Acceptance Criterion A11 (zero-crash fault tolerance under complete external network/provider failure). Attempting to build an offline heuristic NLP answer generator using token overlap is an architectural trap that produces low-accuracy, ungrounded responses.
  *Resolution*: Explicitly state that the tertiary offline mock is a fail-safe escalation engine: it performs deterministic keyword intent matching, retrieves candidate articles from local ChromaDB, and routes to `ESCALATE` with routing reason `"Provider cascade exhausted: offline fail-safe escalation"`. It never attempts speculative automated answer generation.

### Finding 8: Non-Existent Model Identifier & Library Transport Ambiguity
* **Severity:** 🟢 Nit
* **Affected Scope:** Section 6 *Streamlined 3-Tier Provider Cascade* (line 248) and Section 1 *Target Files* (line 318).
* **Simplicity Rationale:** 
  Line 248 lists `openai/gpt-oss-120b` under the Primary Groq API tier. Groq provides `llama-3.3-70b-versatile` and `llama-3.1-8b-instant`, but no model named `openai/gpt-oss-120b`. Additionally, line 318 updates `openai>=1.10.0` for `langchain-openai` without explaining why. Groq endpoints are accessed using the standard OpenAI client via Groq's OpenAI-compatible endpoint (`base_url="https://api.groq.com/openai/v1"`).
  *Resolution*: Replace `openai/gpt-oss-120b` with `llama-3.1-8b-instant` and clarify that the OpenAI SDK serves as the universal client transport for Groq.

---

### Finding 9: Lightweight Policy Enforcement for `must_not_auto_respond`
* **Severity:** 🟢 Nit
* **Affected Scope:** Section *Pipeline State Contract* (lines 200–220) and Section 5 (line 241).
* **Simplicity Rationale:** 
  Comprehension Finding 1 and Critic Finding DES-3 noted that `must_not_auto_respond` is evaluated in the routing gate but undefined in state. Building an elaborate rule engine would violate simplicity.
  *Resolution*: Add `must_not_auto_respond: bool` to `CanonicalTicket` and the pipeline state contract. It is evaluated during normalization/classification by a simple set of deterministic business rules (e.g., ticket requests financial refund, account mutation, or human supervisor).

---

### Finding 10: Section-Based Markdown Chunking without Algorithmic Overkill
* **Severity:** 🟢 Nit
* **Affected Scope:** Section 5 (line 239) and Section *Decisions & Rejected Alternatives* (lines 286–289).
* **Simplicity Rationale:** 
  Comprehension Finding 4 and Critic Finding DES-6 noted ambiguity between "29 articles" and "document chunks". An over-engineered recursive character splitter with dynamic overlap adds unnecessary complexity for 29 structured Markdown articles.
  *Resolution*: Specify Markdown header-based chunking (splitting articles at H2/H3 headers with doc/section anchors), yielding $\sim 70\text{--}90$ focused semantic chunks while maintaining sub-2-second CPU indexing in ChromaDB.

---

### Conclusion & Directives for Document Refinement

The target architecture is sound and minimal at its foundation. To reach production quality:
1. **Purge YAGNI**: Drop MLflow entirely (`evaluation/tracker.py`, `--track` flag) and remove `src/cli.py`.
2. **Clarify Flow & Calibration**: Enforce orthogonal $c$ and $s_{\text{ret}}$ checks; correct linear pipeline notation to show clean conditional branching.
3. **Resist Weeds & Over-Engineering**: Address rate limits via batch pacing and HTTP backoff rather than token-bucket classes; present schemas as concise attribute tables rather than raw code; keep the offline fallback mock strictly biased toward safe escalation.
